One small supermarket, a mountain of cardboard, and the very long road to a store people actually talk about.

01The short version

You take over a small neighbourhood supermarket with more ambition than money. For a while you are the staff: you take the delivery, you stack the shelf, you stand at the till, you mop the floor because somebody dropped a jar of something.

Then the shop starts paying you back. You choose what to sell, who to hire, which patch of ground next door is worth buying — and how much of the work you still want to do with your own hands.

It's a management game with a first-person body. The spreadsheets are real. So are the boxes.

The best moment in any shop sim is the one where you realise you don't have to do this yourself any more.

02What you'll actually be doing

  • Stock it. Order what you sell, and sell what people come back for — including the part where stock has a date on it and yesterday's delivery doesn't sell itself.
  • Build it. Knock a wall through, push the floor out into the land you just bought, and lay the shop out the way you'd want to walk it.
  • Work it. Take deliveries off the truck, get everything onto the right shelf, and run the till when the queue gets long.
  • Staff it. Hire people, train them, schedule them — and learn the very specific pain of someone calling in sick on a Saturday.
  • Run it. Price things, promote things, pay the bills, and make the decisions that only start to matter once rent is due.
  • Grow it. Buy the ground next door, then the ground past that, until the little shop is a name people say.
  • Compete. A shared national market, a daily paper, and your name on a ranking next to everybody else's.

03What I'm not telling you yet

I'd rather this page be short and true than long and decorative. So, plainly:

  • The title. It exists. It isn't final, and it isn't public.
  • The setting and the opening. The first five minutes are the best thing in the design doc and I'm not spending them in a paragraph.
  • Any image. No screenshots, no concept art, no footage. Not until it's ours and current.
  • Most of the mechanics. The ones with numbers attached, especially.

When there's something real to show, it lands on the studio's games page first: primevaentertainment.com/games. That's the only place an announcement will actually appear.

05Built with

  • Unity 6 with URP, built mobile-first. The performance budget gets set on phones before anything is added for PC.
  • First person, with controls designed for touch and then mapped to keyboard and mouse — not the other way round.
  • One shared national layer for the market and the rankings, running independently of whatever you're doing in your own shop.
  • Co-op is planned after launch. Not in the first release. I'd rather say that now than imply otherwise.
